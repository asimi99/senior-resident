Question 14 of 40

A 19-year-old man has 18 hours of abdominal pain that began near the umbilicus and moved to the right lower quadrant. He has nausea and no appetite. He has had no recent cold or sore throat. Temperature is 38.6°C. There is guarding and rebound tenderness at McBurney point. WBC is 16,000/mm³.

Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?

  1. Mesenteric adenitis
  2. Acute appendicitis
  3. Gastroenteritis
  4. Kidney stone

Reading the question
Step 1 of 4 Key finding

The pain began at the umbilicus, then moved to the right lower quadrant. What does the move mean?

AIt is spreading across the abdomen
BThe lining over one spot is now irritated
CIt is referred from the ureter
Not quiteSpreading pain gets wider. This pain got more exact.
CorrectVague midline pain is visceral. Sharp pain in one spot means the lining there is inflamed.
Step 2 of 4 Distractor

He is nauseated with no appetite. How much weight does that carry?

AA lot. It points to a gut infection
BLittle. The pain came first
CA lot. It suggests obstruction
CorrectPain, then nausea. When vomiting leads and pain follows, think gut infection.
